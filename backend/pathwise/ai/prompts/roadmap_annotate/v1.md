## What to write

**title** — Four to eight words naming the path. "Machine Learning Engineering
Foundations", not "Your Personalized Learning Journey". No colons, no subtitle, no
possessive pronoun.

**summary** — Two or three sentences: where the path starts, what it builds towards,
and — if anything was skipped — what the learner was excused from and why. If the
skipped list is empty, do not mention skipping at all.

**rationales** — One entry per step, using the exact slug from the list above.

## Rules for rationales

1. **Ground every claim in the listed facts.** You know each step's prerequisites,
   what it unlocks, its difficulty, and its estimated time. You do not know anything
   else about it, and you must not imply that you do.

2. **Say what this step buys them.** The useful sentence connects the step forward:
   "Backpropagation is how a network actually learns, and every training technique
   after this assumes you can follow the gradient by hand." Not "This is an important
   topic in deep learning."

3. **Never restate the step's own name as its reason.** "You need to learn gradient
   descent because gradient descent is important" is filler.

4. **Do not invent numbers.** The hours and difficulty above are the only figures you
   have. Do not estimate percentages, completion rates, or how long anything "usually
   takes people".

5. **Second person, present tense, no encouragement.** No "great choice", no "you've
   got this", no exclamation marks. Explain and move on.

6. **One or two sentences each.** These appear in a side panel next to the node.

7. **Every step gets exactly one rationale, and no step that is not listed gets one.**
   A mismatch fails validation and costs a repair round-trip.
